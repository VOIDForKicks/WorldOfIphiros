##### Description/Background:
Kelemvor, once a mortal mercenary captain cursed to transform into a panther-like beast whenever he performed a noble deed, ascended to godhood at the conclusion of the Avatar Crisis (the Time of Troubles). He assumed the mantle of the Lord of the Dead following the destruction of the mad god Cyric and the fall of the previous, tyrannical death gods Myrkul and Bane. Unlike his predecessors who ruled the Underworld through terror, sadism, or torturous decay, Kelemvor revolutionized the office of death by bringing absolute fairness, order, and compassion to the afterlife. He views death not as a punishment, but as a natural, necessary culmination of mortal life. In his domain of the Fugue Plane, he judges souls with strict, unvarnished objectivity, ensuring they are sent to the correct resting places of their respective patrons. He harbours a deep, burning hatred for the undead and those who extend their lifespans artificially through necromancy, viewing such practices as an abominable disruption of the cosmic balance. He is typically depicted as a stern, solemn, yet deeply empathetic knight clad in dark, silver-trimmed armour, carrying a massive bastard sword.
##### Worshippers:
Morticians, grave diggers, solemn paladins, judges, and hunters of the undead. His temples, often called Comforting Houses, are usually located adjacent to grand cemeteries or catacombs; they are quiet, peaceful structures where the living can mourn and the dying are provided with painless, dignified transitions.
##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets:
- Respect the Natural End: Do not fear death, nor seek to delay it artificially beyond its natural time; celebrate a life well-lived and help others find peace in their final moments. 
- Eradicate the Undead: Hunt down and destroy zombies, liches, vampires, and all other unholy mockeries of life that seek to cheat the grave; their existence is a scar upon the multiverse. 
- Judge with Equity: Treat the souls of the high-born and the low-born with equal fairness; let no personal bias, wealth, or mortal status tilt the scales of ultimate justice.
