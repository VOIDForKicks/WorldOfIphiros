##### Description/Background
Ilmater, known across Toril as the One Who Endures or the Broken God, is the absolute embodiment of selfless compassion, sacrifice, and quiet fortitude. Unlike deities who demand conquest or boast of physical perfection, Ilmater chooses to take the collective agony, torment, and misery of the multiverse onto his own shoulders. He is depicted as a heavily scarred man with broken joints, gnarled hands, and an expression of profound, gentle sorrow. He does not suffer out of despair; rather, his suffering is an active, protective shield meant to alleviate the burdens of the fragile, the oppressed, and the tortured.

His mythology is defined by his unwavering role as the ultimate protector of the helpless. Whenever a mortal undergoes immense physical or psychological trauma, Ilmater enters their mind or spirit to share the pain, giving them the supernatural willpower needed to survive. He is a steadfast ally of Tyr and Torm, forming the Triad, a coalition dedicated to absolute justice, duty, and protective law. He fiercely despises cruelty in all forms, placing his followers directly in opposition to sadistic tyrants, torturers, and malignant entities who derive joy from the degradation of others.
##### Worshippers
Paladins of devotion or redemption, life domain clerics, battlefield medics, ascetics, political political prisoners, and champions of the poor. His holy spaces are not opulent cathedrals but humble, clean infirmaries, roadside shelters, and underground asylums where the sick and broken are treated without cost or judgment.
##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets
- Bear the Burden of Others: Stand willingly between the vulnerable and those who wish to cause them harm; if someone must endure pain or punishment to save an innocent, let it be you.
- Provide Unconditional Comfort: Never turn away a soul in genuine agony, regardless of their past or alignment; tend to their wounds, ease their distress, and offer them a path to recovery.
- Endure Without Bitterness: Meet cruelty, torture, and systemic oppression with unyielding internal fortitude; do not let the malice of the world poison your capacity for empathy and mercy.
