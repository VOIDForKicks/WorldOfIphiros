##### Description/Background
Flidais (often referred to as Flidas) is the ancient Celtic goddess of wild animals, woodlands, and domestic herds. In Irish folklore, she represents a fascinating bridge between untamed nature and early pastoral civilization. She is famously associated with a legendary herd of magical cattle and deer; her primary beast, the _Maol Flidais_, was said to yield enough rich milk in a single milking to sustain an entire army for a week.

Her domestic role is heavily contrasted by her wild, shape-shifting nature and her choice of consorts, which included high-ranking kings and elemental heroes like Fergus mac Róich. Flidais is often envisioned riding in a magnificent chariot pulled by swift, powerful stags rather than horses. While deities like Cernunnos represent the primal hunt and the cycle of death, Flidais embodies the nurturing, sustaining aspect of the wilderness, the absolute assurance that even in the depths of the forest or the height of a harsh season, the earth provides deep sustenance to those who respect its creatures.
##### Worshippers
Dairy farmers, frontier cattle ranchers, circle of the land or shepherd druids, wilderness scouts, nomadic survivalists, and life domain clerics. Her shrines are typically rustic, wooden barns or open-air woodland pastures where wild deer and livestock commingle peacefully, and where her followers leave offerings of fresh milk, sweet hay, and wild berries.
##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets
- **Sustain the Community:** Use the bounty of nature and livestock to ensure that no innocent person goes hungry; starvation is a failure of pastoral stewardship.
- **Respect the Beast:** Protect herds and wild fauna from needless cruelty or wasteful slaughter; a beast must be cared for if it is to care for you.
- **Bridge the Wild and the Tame:** Act as a mediator between expanding settlements and the deep forests, ensuring that civilization takes only what it needs without choking out wildlife.
