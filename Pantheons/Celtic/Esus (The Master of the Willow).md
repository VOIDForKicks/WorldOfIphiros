##### Description/Background
Esus (meaning "Lord" or "Master") is a highly prominent, deeply enigmatic deity from continental Gaulish Celtic mythology. Alongside Taranis and Teutates, he formed a powerful triad of gods widely feared and respected throughout ancient Europe. He is uniquely depicted on the historical _Pillar of the Boatmen_ monument as a robust, muscular woodsman systematically pruning or felling a sacred willow tree with an axe, situated directly adjacent to a mystical bull carrying three cranes upon its back (_Tarvos Trigaranus_).

This ritual woodcutting represents a profound cosmic exchange: pruning the branches of the world tree to stimulate new growth, clearing away the old to cultivate the fertile soil, and extracting raw resources for human advancement. However, classical Roman historians noted that Esus demanded a strict, brutal price for his elemental blessings. His sacrificial victims were hung from trees and methodically lashed or lacerated until the patterns of their dripping blood revealed prophetic omens to the attendant druids. He stands as a stark, gritty reminder that the wilderness is a place of harsh transaction; to build, create, or survive, something must always be given up in return.
##### Worshippers
Loggers, lumber barons, wilderness merchants, carpenters, structural architects, circle of the land druids, and rangers who clear land for expanding civilizations. His shrines are functional timber yards or massive stump clearings deep within old forests, decorated with hanging leather strips and skulls, where offerings of iron tools and high-quality lumber are dedicated before any forest clearing begins.
##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets
- Honour the Fair Exchange: Take nothing from the world without offering a payment of equal or greater value; respect the material and spiritual cost of your survival.    
- Prune the Weakness: Clear away deadwood, corrupt ideas, and failing structures within yourself and your community to make room for vibrant new growth.
- Master the Craft of the Wild: View trees and natural resources not merely as holy monuments, but as gifts to be masterfully harvested, shaped, and utilized to forge lasting civilization.
