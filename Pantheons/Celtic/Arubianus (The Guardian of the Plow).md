Lesser Deity
##### Description/Background:
Arubianus is a deity whose worship was rooted in the Gallo-Roman regions of ancient Europe, particularly tied to fertile river valleys and farming communities. As a god of agriculture, his domain is the delicate balance of the seasons, turning wild, untamed wilderness into life-sustaining, bountiful soil.

Unlike gods of untamed nature, Arubianus represents the _relationship_ between human civilization and the earth. He is the patron of the plowman, the sower, and the reaper. In ancient art, he is often associated with traditional farming tools, most notably the sickle, which signifies both the labour of the harvest and the protective boundary against famine. He is a quiet, stoic deity who values patience, hard work, and cyclical harmony over grand spectacles.
##### Worshippers: 
Farmers, peasants, homesteaders, and druids who protect rural frontiers. His shrines are typically humble, open-air stone altars built at the edges of tilled fields, often adorned with the first fruits of a seasonal harvest.
##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets:
 - Nurture the Soil: Protect the land from corruption, blight, and unholy desecration.
- Feed the Community: Ensure that the fruits of labour are shared fairly and that no one falls to starvation.
- Respect the Cycle: Accept that life, death, and rebirth are natural phases; everything has its season.
