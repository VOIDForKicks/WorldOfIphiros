##### Description/Background
Danu (also known as Dana) is the foundational maternal deity of the Irish Celtic pantheon, serving as the cosmic mother figure from whom the entire divine race, the **Tuatha Dé Danann** (The Tribes of the Goddess Danu), draws their lineage, magical essence, and name. She represents the ancient, nurturing spirit of the earth itself, the life-giving flow of major river systems, and the cosmic waters of wisdom that fed the roots of the world before time was recorded.

Because her origin predates the structured prose of later mythological cycles, she remains an ethereal, omnipresent force rather than a localized character with personal squabbles. Danu represents absolute abundance, the raw fertility required to sustain life through brutal seasons, and the profound ancestral magic that separates the divine from the mundane. She is the literal land beneath the mortals' feet and the water that cleanses their wounds, standing as a tranquil counterweight to the blood-soaked warfare that dominates much of Celtic folklore.
##### Worshippers
Circle of the land druids, life domain clerics, earth genasi, centaurs, ancient elven matriarchs, peaceful agricultural communities, and explorers charting ancient river deltas. Her shrines are completely open-air sanctuaries situated at the physical confluence of major rivers, deep inside primordial cave springs, or upon the crests of rolling green hills where milk, honey, and flawless emeralds are poured directly into the soil as offerings.
##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets
- Nurture the Source: Protect the pure springs, pristine rivers, and fertile fields of the world from unnatural corruption, systematic defilement, or blights.
- Honour the Lineage: Respect your ancestors and guide the younger generations with patience and wisdom; ensure that the history and magical traditions of your people are never forgotten.
- Provide Unconditional Shelter: Open your doors and offer sustenance to those displaced by war, famine, or tragedy, acting as a gentle, providing hand to the weary.
