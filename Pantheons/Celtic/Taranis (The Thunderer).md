##### Description/Background: 
Taranis is an ancient and powerful Gaulish god whose name translates directly to "The Thunderer." Alongside Esus and Teutates, he formed a powerful triad of deities highly revered across ancient Gaul, Britain, and Iberia. Taranis represents the raw, booming authority of the celestial heavens, wielding lightning bolts to strike down cosmic threats and maintain the rotational order of the universe. He is traditionally depicted as a bearded, robust warrior god holding a thunderbolt in one hand and his sacred spoked wheel in the other. In Celtic archeology, this wheel symbolizes both the motion of the sun across the sky and the rolling thunder of his heavenly chariot. Roman chroniclers frequently associated him with Jupiter due to his dominion over storms, though his Celtic worship carried a more primal, elemental edge, historically associated with great bonfires and sacrifices meant to appease the dangerous fury of seasonal tempests.
##### Worshippers: 
Sailors, mountain dwellers, siege engineers, and tempest-driven druids or barbarians. His shrines are typically constructed on high mountain peaks, wind-swept ridges, or open plains where the sky dominates the horizon and lightning strikes are frequent.
##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets: 
- Command the Storm: Channel the overwhelming force of the elements to shatter your enemies; let your presence be as undeniable as a thunderclap. 
- Uphold the Great Wheel: Respect the natural and cosmic cycles of the world, ensuring that neither absolute chaos nor stagnant law disrupts the turning of the ages. 
- Fear No Sky: Stand tall beneath the raging tempest and show no fear in the face of natural disaster; the storm is a reminder of divine power, not a shelter to hide from.
