##### Description/Background:
Maponos (whose name literally translates to "Great Son" or "Divine Son") is an ancient Celtic god of youth, vibrant vitality, and acoustic beauty. He was widely worshipped across ancient Gaul and Roman Britain, often conflated by the Romans with Apollo due to his striking handsome features, connection to healing thermal springs, and absolute mastery over stringed instruments.

In later Welsh mythology, he is recognizable as the legendary figure Mabon ap Modron (Mabon, Son of Mother). His myth is deeply symbolic of the natural world: snatched from his mother when he was only three nights old, he was imprisoned for ages in the dark fortress of Gloucester until he was famously rescued by King Arthur’s knights with the aid of the oldest animals in the world. This imprisonment and rescue serve as an allegory for the seasonal cycle, representing how the warmth of youth and summer is captured by winter's darkness, only to be triumphantly reborn. He is a joyful, expressive deity who infuses the mortal realm with the therapeutic energy of song.
##### Worshippers: 
Bards, traveling musicians, young hunters, entertainers, and those seeking rejuvenation or healing. His shrines are typically vibrant places, sunlit amphitheatres, restorative hot springs, or communal halls filled with art and dance.
##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets:
- Keep the Song Alive: Foster creativity and music wherever you go; art is the ultimate defence against the despair of the world.
- Break the Chains: Fiercely oppose wrongful imprisonment, captivity, and the suppression of free will.
- Protect the Dawn: Defend the innocent hopefulness of youth and the natural cycles of growth against unnatural corruption.
