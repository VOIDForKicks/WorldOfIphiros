##### Description/Background:
Aengus (also known as _Aengus Óg_ or _Oengus of the Bruigh_) is the Celtic god of youth, beauty, passion, and poetic inspiration. Born of the Dagda and Boann, his conception is steeped in myth, his parents halted the sun for nine months so he could be conceived and born on the exact same day.

He is traditionally depicted as a radiantly handsome young man surrounded by four singing birds that circle his head. In lore, these birds represent his kisses turned into song, capable of inspiring profound love or deep sorrow in those who hear them. Aengus is a master of wit and trickery, famously using clever wordplay to outsmart his father and claim ownership of Newgrange (_Bru na Bóinne_), his magnificent sacred mound.

He is deeply associated with swans due to the famous tale of Caer Ibormeith, a woman who transformed into a swan every alternating year. Aengus fell in love with her in a dream, sought her out, and chose to transform into a swan himself so they could fly away together.
##### Worshippers: 
Bards, young lovers, diplomats, and free-spirited sorcerers. His shrines are often found in sunlit forest glades, by serene lakes populated by waterfowl, or within ancient stone mounds.
##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets:
Followers of Aengus swear to protect the innocent joys of youth, nurture artistic expression, and defend true love against tyranny or arranged stagnation. They prefer clever tricks over brute force to solve conflicts.
