##### Description/Background:
In the Irish mythological cycles, Neit (also spelled _Néit_) is an ancient, primordial god of war and violent conflict. Unlike tactical deities who focus strictly on chivalry or defensive honor, Neit embodies the chaotic, terrifying rush of the battlefield itself. He is a prominent figure among the Tuatha Dé Danann, famously married to two terrifying war goddesses: Nemain (the personification of battlefield panic and frenzy) and Badb (the crow goddess who foretells death). In some texts, he is also linked directly to the Morrígan.

Neit's existence is defined by eternal strife. He was killed during the legendary Second Battle of Mag Tuired, fighting against the monstrous Fomorians. He represents the stark reality of Celtic warfare, furious charges, clashing metal, the absolute chaos of combat, and the grim pride of warriors. He does not care who wins a war; he cares only that it is fought with unyielding ferocity and courage.
##### Worshippers: 
Barbarians, mercenaries, frontline fighters, and bloodthirsty warlords. His shrines are rarely found in peaceful towns. Instead, they are erected hastily on the eve of battle using piled shields and weapons, or built inside active military fortresses and mercenary compounds.
##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets:
- Face the Foe: Never show your back to an enemy. Cowardice is the ultimate betrayal of the spark of battle.
- Embrace the Frenzy: Let the passion of combat flow through you. Do not overthink the battlefield; rely on your instincts, your strength, and your steel.
- Honour the Fallen: Give a warrior's respect to those who fight bravely, whether they are allies or mortal enemies.
