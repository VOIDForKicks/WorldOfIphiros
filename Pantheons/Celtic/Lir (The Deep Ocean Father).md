##### Description/Background:
Lir (also known as _Ler_, meaning "The Sea") is the primordial personification of the ocean in Irish mythology. Rather than just a god who rules over the water, Lir is often treated as the vast, untamed ocean itself. He is a primary figure among the Tuatha Dé Danann, famously known as the tragic father in the heart-breaking tale _The Children of Lir_, where his four beautiful children are cursed by a jealous stepmother to live as swans across the cold lakes and stormy seas of Ireland for 900 years.

Lir represents the unpredictable, dual nature of the deep ocean, immeasurably generous one day, providing bountiful fishing and safe passage, and devastatingly cruel the next, swallowing entire fleets in a sudden fit of rage. He is an ancient, brooding deity who rarely involves himself in the petty politics of the material world, preferring the quiet, eternal expanse of the abyssal depths. His more famous son, Manannán mac Lir, acts as the active guardian of the shores, while Lir remains the master of the deep.
##### Worshippers: 
Sailors, fishermen, ocean-dwelling races (like Tritons, Sea Elves, or Water Genasi), and coast-dwelling druids. Shrines to Lir are rarely grand temples; they are usually natural rocky promontories jutting into the sea, where offerings of silver or valuable trade goods are cast directly into the waves.
##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets:
- Respect the Void: Acknowledge the terrifying, vast power of the ocean. Never take its bounty without offering thanks or sacrifice.
- Protect the Waters: Cleanse the seas of unnatural, abyssal aberrations, krakens, or magical pollutions that threaten the balance of marine life.
- Endure and Flow: Like the tide, be willing to shift, adapt, and weather any storm thrown against you. What recedes will always crash back.
