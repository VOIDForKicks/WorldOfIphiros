##### Description/Background
Dionysus is the vibrant Olympian god of wine, theatre, and ritual ecstasy. As the son of Zeus and the mortal princess Semele, his birth was plagued by Hera's jealousy; Semele was consumed by Zeus's true divine radiance, prompting Zeus to rescue the unborn infant and sew him into his own thigh until he was ready to be born. This mythic origin earned him the title "Twice-Born."

Dionysus represents both the dual nature of wine: liberating, joyful, and creatively inspiring on one hand, but dangerously unhinged, blinding, and destructive on the other. He spent his youth wandering the mortal world, spreading the secrets of viticulture and gathering a wild retinue of satyrs and frenzied women known as Maenads. Unlike the more rigid Olympians, Dionysus is a god of the common people, breaking down social barriers and freeing minds from societal constraints. However, he is fiercely vindictive against those who deny his divinity or preach excessive restraint. He famously drove King Pentheus of Thebes mad, leading to the king being torn limb from limb by his own mother in a blind, drunken fury, a stark reminder of the terrifying power of ritual madness.
##### Worshippers
Bards, actors, brewers, tavern keepers, satyrs, hedge wizards, and free-spirited rebels who reject strict societal hierarchies. His temples are rarely grand cathedrals; instead, they are lively open-air amphitheatres, hidden woodland groves where wild midnight revels take place, or the storage cellars of prestigious city vineyards.

##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets
- Embrace Liberation: Break the chains of rigid, unfeeling dogma; encourage individuals to find joy, express their inner truth, and indulge in the pleasures of life.
- Honour the Performance: Treat the arts, theatre, and storytelling as sacred crafts that can unmask hypocrisy and reveal deep truths through illusion and song.
- Beware the Excess: Understand the fine line between divine ecstasy and self-destructive madness; indulge freely, but do not let the wine consume your mind entirely.
