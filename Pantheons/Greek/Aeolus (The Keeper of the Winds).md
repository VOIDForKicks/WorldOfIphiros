##### Description/Background: 
Aeolus is the divine keeper of the winds in Greek mythology, residing on the floating, cliff-bound island of Aeolia. Rather than being a primordial force of the sky himself, he was granted absolute authority by Zeus to control, contain, and unleash the four directional winds, Boreas (North), Zephyrus (West), Notus (South), and Eurus (East), which he keeps locked securely away inside deep, subterranean cavern vaults or within a specialized ox-hide container. Aeolus is famously known for his appearance in Homer's _Odyssey_, where he warmly hosted Odysseus and gifted him a tightly bound bag containing all the chaotic storm-winds of the world, leaving only a gentle western breeze to guide the hero's ship home. When Odysseus’s curious crew foolishly ripped the bag open right before reaching Ithaca, the violent gale blasted them all the way back to Aeolia. Viewing their misfortune as a clear sign of divine abandonment and a curse from the Olympian gods, a stern Aeolus promptly cast them off his island without mercy.
##### Worshippers: 
Sailors, navigators, air genasi, griffon riders, and coastal merchants whose livelihoods rely completely on favourable atmospheric conditions. Shrines dedicated to him are typically open-air structures built atop high, wind-swept bluffs or integrated directly into the crow's nests and figureheads of long-distance sailing vessels.
##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets:

- Maintain the Vault: Keep your volatile emotions and destructive impulses firmly locked away, unleashing them only with calculated precision when the situation demands a storm.
- Honour the Chosen Course: Do not squander divine favour or path-clearing opportunities through greed or curiosity; see a journey through to its destination with an unwavering focus.
