##### Description/Background
Demeter is the Olympian goddess of the harvest, agriculture, and the cyclical nature of life and death. The daughter of the Titans Cronus and Rhea, she possesses absolute control over the fertility of the soil, making her one of the most vital deities for mortal survival.

She is most famously known for the myth of the abduction of her daughter, Persephone, by Hades. Consumed by inconsolable grief and rage, Demeter withdrew her blessings from the world, causing a massive, catastrophic famine where the earth grew cold and nothing could grow. To prevent humanity's extinction, Zeus intervened, negotiating a deal where Persephone would spend a portion of the year in the Underworld and the rest with her mother. This separation directly accounts for the changing seasons: when Persephone returns to the Underworld, Demeter grieves and the earth falls into winter; when she returns to Mount Olympus, Demeter rejoices, bringing forth the blossoming warmth of spring and summer. Demeter also established the Eleusinian Mysteries, secretive and highly revered religious rites that promised initiates profound blessings and a peaceful afterlife.
##### Worshippers
Farmers, peasants, druids, community leaders, and life domain clerics who nurture civilizations from the ground up. Her temples are typically grand, rustic stone complexes situated near rich valleys, community granaries, or sprawling wheat fields, heavily decorated with seasonal fruits and ears of corn.
##### Cleric/Paladin Tenets
- Sustain the Hearth: Protect the fields, orchards, and livestock that feed your community; understand that true societal power begins with a well-nourished population.
- Respect the Seasons: Embrace the natural cycles of abundance and scarcity, using times of harvest to prepare diligently for the inevitable, cold winters of life.
- Defend the Innocent: Stand fiercely in defense of family ties and the vulnerable; let your wrath be absolute against tyrants who rip loved ones apart or destroy the livelihood of the working commoner.
