## 1. Establish the "Paper Trail" (The Meta-Information)
Before the players read a single word of the letter, they should learn something about the world just by looking at it or hearing how you describe it.
- **The Medium:** Is it expensive parchment, cheap scrap paper, a torn piece of sailcloth, or a magical projection stone?
- **The Ink/Instrument:** Is it written in neat, expensive ink? Charcoal? Dried blood? Is the handwriting a frantic scrawl or elegant calligraphy?
- **The Seal:** Wax seals are the ultimate faction identifiers. If they find a letter sealed with the crest of a royal house they thought was extinct, that’s an instant mystery.
## 2. Match the NPC’s Voice (The "Voice-to-Page" Rule)
An illiterate goblin chief, a paranoid wizard, and a snooty elven noble should never sound the same on paper.
- **The Orator:** If the NPC is formal, use long, winding sentences and flowery vocabulary. Avoid contractions (_"do not"_ instead of _"don't"_).
- **The Pragmatist:** Keep sentences short, blunt, and to the point. No fluff.
- **The Chaotic/Panicked:** Use run-on sentences, crossed-out words, ink splatters, or frantic postscripts (P.S.).
- **The Time-Traveller:** If the NPC is from another era, use slightly archaic (or suspiciously futuristic) slang, or refer to historical events in the wrong tense (e.g., _"I write this on the eve of the Great Collapse, which I believe you call 'Tuesday'"_).
## 3. The "Two Truths and a Question" Rule (Pacing Info)
NPC letters are lore-delivery vehicles, but they shouldn't be massive "lore dumps." To keep players engaged, every letter should ideally contain:
1. **Truth #1 (The Hook):** A clear piece of information they can use right now (e.g., _"The guard captain is taking bribes."_).
2. **Truth #2 (The Context):** A piece of worldbuilding or a clue to a larger plot (e.g., _"He is being paid in ancient coins from a timeline that shouldn't exist."_).
3. **The Question (The Mystery):** A lingering hook or cliff-hanger that forces the players to act or investigate (e.g., _"But who is minting them?"_).
## 4. Write for "Selective Damage" (The DM's Cheat Code)
If you want to give players a clue but aren't ready for them to have the whole answer yet, design the letter with built-in "damage."
- State in your description that a portion of the letter is **burned, water-damaged, torn, or stained with blood**.
- Write the full letter first, then black out or tear away the key names, dates, or locations.
 **Example:** _"Meet me at the ruins of [BLOTTED OUT BY BLOOD] before the clock strikes [BURNED AWAY]. Bring the artefact."_
## 5. Use the "P.S." for Dramatic Punch
In real life, people use a _Postscript_ (P.S.) when they forget something. In D&D, the P.S. is your best tool for dropping a sudden twist, a personal threat, or a moment of dark humour that sticks with the players.
- _The Threat:_ _"P.S. I know about the Tiefling. Tell him his past is catching up."_
- _The Urgency:_ _"P.S. If you smell ozone, burn this letter immediately."_
- _The Character Beat:_ _"P.S. Tell Hugh I still have his favourite dagger."_
