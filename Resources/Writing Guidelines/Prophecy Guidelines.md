## 1. The Golden Rule: Prophesy the _Vibe_, Not the _Mechanics_
Never prophesy specific mechanical actions or highly specific narrative choices. If you say, _"The paladin shall strike the red dragon with the Holy Avenger on the third dawn,"_ your paladin will inevitably die, lose the sword, or decide to make peace with the dragon.
- **Bad (Too Specific):** _"Xeno will stab the Lich King with the silver dagger of ruin."_
- **Good (Vague & Evocative):** _"A silver blade, wielded by a hand untouched by time, shall pierce the shadow’s heart."_ (This could apply to Xeno, a rogue, a different silver weapon, or even a metaphorical "blade" like a spell).
## 2. Use the "Rule of Three" Structure
A great prophecy usually has three distinct parts. This makes it feel like a complete riddle for the players to solve over the course of the campaign.
- **Part 1: The Catalyst (The past/present sign).** How do they know the prophecy has begun?
    - _e.g., "When the red star weeps into the sea..."_
- **Part 2: The Condition (The obstacle/quest).** What must be done or gathered?
    - _e.g., "...three souls bound by fractured time must stand where the sky fell..."_
- **Part 3: The Crossroads (The double-edged outcome).** What happens at the end? (Crucially, always frame this with an _either/or_ or a warning, leaving the outcome in the players' hands).
    - _e.g., "...to forge a new dawn, or sink the world into eternal night."_
## 3. Build in "Cheats" and Loop-holes
The best prophecies in fiction are solved by clever interpretations of the words (think Shakespeare’s Macbeth and "no man of woman born"). Build these loopholes into your wording so your players can feel like geniuses when they figure them out—or so you can adapt when they do something unexpected.
- **Literal vs. Metaphorical:** If the prophecy mentions a "crown," it could be a gold helmet, a constellation, a bloodline, or a tavern called _The King's Head_.
- **Double Meanings:** Words like _fall, rise, break, turn,_ and _light_ can mean physical actions or emotional/political shifts.
## 4. Tailor It to Your Campaign Themes (e.g., Time Travel)
Since your campaign involves time travel and temporal anomalies, play into that! Prophecies in a time-travel campaign shouldn't just be about the future—they can be memories of a past that hasn't happened yet, or echoes of a timeline that was erased.
- **The "Closed Loop" Prophecy:** The prophecy is actually a historical record of what the players _already did_ in the future, sent back to the past.
- **The "Fading Ink" Prophecy:** As the players change the timeline, the words of the prophecy physically shift on the page.
- **Temporal Synonyms:** Use time-related imagery instead of directional ones. Use words like _tether, anchor, echo, thread, needle, knot, ripple,_ or _stich_.
## 5. Keep a "Translation Key" in Your DM Notes
When you write a prophecy, immediately write down your **intended** meaning next to it. However, write it in pencil. If your players come up with a theory that is cooler or fits better than your original plan, **steal their idea** and pretend it was your plan all along.

| The Prophecy Line                           | Your Original Meaning                             | What It Could _Also_ Mean (If the players derail)                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _"Three steps backward, one leap ahead..."_ | A puzzle involving stepping tiles.                | Time-traveling back three centuries to change one pivotal event in the present.                    |
